# Voice and form

Items marked **(DM, ...)** restate an instruction or correction the DM gave, with where it came from (the original brief, or a session's review). Everything else is style guidance distilled from the published chapters; it may be revised, and it must never be presented to the DM as their rule.

## The chapter form

Match the previous chapter's `story.md` exactly. The skeleton:

```
# <Title>

Gentle reader, <one or two paragraphs that set a theme the chapter will earn>

## I. <Section title>

<prose>

## II. <Section title>

...

<optional closing: where the company stands as Volo writes, "Mark ..." lines>

*V.G.*
```

**(DM, s32)** No preface or header between the title and "Gentle reader"; the chapter is a continuation and starts in the story. Six to eight roman-numbered sections. **(DM, s32)** When the session ends on a cliffhanger, the chapter ends on it: the last line of the scene, then the sign-off, with no closing reflection. The next chapter picks up there. A closing passage belongs only to a chapter whose session reached a resting point. Plain paragraphs separated by one blank line. `*italics*` only in the sign-off and rare emphasis; no other markdown. No em-dashes anywhere: colons, commas and full stops carry the rhythm. Numbers are words, except years (1385, 1494). The story picks up at the previous chapter's final beat and does not re-introduce anything the chronicle has already introduced.

## Volo's voice

- A narrator who was not there and says so: "I am told", "the company remembered", "I record it because". He has read the testimony and has opinions about it.
- Confident, wry, digressive for a clause and then back to business. One or two asides about Volo's own misadventures per chapter, never inside a fight.
- Long sentences built on colons and commas, then a short one that lands.
- **(DM, brief)** The characters are the heroes of an action novel. Name them, show what they did, and let the deed carry the weight. The second-reference pattern follows the published chapters: Vex, then "the dragonborn"; Urida, "the gnome"; Grieg, "the wulven"; Chumble, "the goblin"; Lilac Mist, "the tabaxi".
- **(DM, s32)** Volo sees the world, not the game. A die result is what it did: the arrow finds the throat, the fireball leaves one standing. Never the number, never hit points, armor class, concentration, saving throws, spell slots, damage types, rests or turns. A spell is the thing it does (the pale dome of force, the floating light) unless the chronicle already names it.
- Avoid modern idiom in Volo's narration (fast-forward, okay, basically) even where the table used it. Style guidance, not a ruling.
- **(DM, s32)** When the company consults Volo's Guide to Monsters, keep only what bears on their decision (the wight's drain, and whether it heals); the DM cut a summary of the monster's other rules.
- **(DM, s32)** A line spoken at the table may be a player's joke rather than the character's words (Grieg asking the ghost whether she had heard of Artus Cimber was one). When the transcript shows laughter, or the line would be out of character, treat it as table talk and leave it out.
- **(DM, s32)** Table talk is not testimony. An observation the players made about the game (which kill came last, what a monster's rules do, a tally) stays out unless a character would plausibly have thought it in the moment.
- **(DM, s32)** Do not have Volo profess surprise at an NPC's phrasing when it is natural for who they are: a mage hunter of the Obanashi would have nicknames for the undead. Report the phrase.

## Dialogue

**(DM, s32)** The transcript fixes what was said. The speaker owns the words.

- **(DM, s32)** Quote the obvious spoken beats: an NPC's answer, a pledge, a ghost's request, a voice in the dark. Narrate the rest.
- **(DM, s32)** Recast every quote in the character's own voice and register, keeping its substance. Table idiom goes: you folks, lean into, pretty good, kind of, I guess, stuff. Long quotes especially must not sound like the DM improvising. The registers used so far (Mesika formal, a soldier of old Mezro; Artus dry and tired; Xhosala clipped; Aviline ancient and courtly) passed review; adjust them as the DM corrects them.
- **(DM, brief)** Never invent a line that is not in the transcript. Narrate those beats instead. This includes extending a real line: if the transcript has "squash these bugs", the recast may not add "then we see to the wight". Every quotation in the draft has a line in `quotes.md` with its timestamp; the ledger is how the writer proves it to themselves.
- **(DM, s32)** Never announce that a quote is exact. Use it as a novelist would.
- **(DM, s32)** Every quote sits on the question or statement it answered at the table. Include the prompt, in narration, or cut the line.
- Short comic lines may stay as spoken (Wickles: "Hey, hey, I've got a fresh pickle over here").
- **(DM, s32)** Player characters are protagonists, never anonymous extras. Not "somebody wondered aloud". Name the character, leave the speaker unsaid ("Perhaps there was some magical entity controlling her"), or, sparingly, credit the whole company ("our heroes mused that...").

## What the reader may know

Only what the company learned, told as the company understood it.

- **(DM, s32)** Dragonbait's scents: the party has not decoded them. Write the scent and, if Artus read it aloud, his reading. Never the meaning, and no aside about the company not knowing it.
- **(DM, brief; ruling s30)** Names, identities and motives the company never heard stay out: the spirit naga's name, who guards the heart, anything from DM notes.
- **(DM, s32)** Module read-aloud is for description, and a phrase from it is not a name the reader knows; the DM asked where "the hall of scorched walls" came from, and it came from the module, not the chronicle. The reader knows "the temple at the Seething Halls", not "the hall of scorched walls".
- **(DM, s32)** Every motive, arrangement, location and timeline needs a source in the log, the chronicle, `facts.md` or a ruling; the DM corrected an invented reason for Vorn waiting outside the city and an invented setting for the Ring of Winter's first use. If no source says, ask. Do not supply a plausible reason.
- **(DM, s32)** Realms lore from the novels, the wikis and general knowledge is welcome and should be used: Volo would know it. It must be real lore, not invention, and it yields to the chronicle and to `facts.md` where they differ.
- Narration is bound by the same sourcing as dialogue. "He had said as much on the road" is a claim about the past, and if no log, chronicle or transcript line says it, it is invented, however natural it sounds. Two test drafts added that exact clause; neither had a source.
- **(DM, s32)** Know where each concrete detail about a character, creature or place comes from: the chronicle, the session's sources, the bestiary entry or the module text. Check them before writing the detail, and check them all again before answering the DM's question about it. Dragonbait's holy avenger and shield are in his stat block; the colors I once gave the shield were in nothing.

## Rationalizations to refuse

| Thought | Reality |
|---|---|
| "The paraphrase is close enough" | It loses the beat. Recast the real line in the speaker's voice. |
| "The character would have said this" | Then the transcript would have it. A fitting line that is not there is invented. |
| "I'll flag the inference in my report and write it anyway" | A flagged guess in the prose is still a guess in the prose. Leave it out until the DM answers. |
| "The DM's words are the quote" | They fix the content, not the wording. The character did not say "you folks". |
| "The module says the walls are scorched, so the reader knows the hall" | The reader knows the chronicle, not the module. |
| "A plausible reason reads better than none" | An invented motive is a factual error the DM will catch. Ask or omit. |
| "'Somebody asked' is harmless" | It demotes a protagonist to an extra. |
| "The scent meanings are in the module" | The party has not decoded them. The reader may not either. |
| "Hit points make the danger concrete" | Volo cannot see hit points. Describe the wound. |
| "A little framing shows the quote is real" | The reader assumes it is. Framing breaks the voice. |

## Checklist before publishing a review page

- [ ] Title, "Gentle reader", roman sections, `*V.G.*`. No header between title and opening.
- [ ] `check_story.py` reports nothing.
- [ ] Every quote has a timestamp in `quotes.md`, is recast in the speaker's voice without added content, and sits on its prompt.
- [ ] No party member reduced to "somebody", "someone" or "one of them".
- [ ] Every motive, location and timeline has a source; geography checked against `facts.md`.
- [ ] Nothing the company has not learned; no scent meanings.
- [ ] Opens where the previous chapter closed; nothing already introduced is re-introduced.
- [ ] Real names: none, from the transcript or anywhere else.
