# The publisher

The publisher reads every entry before it goes out and rejects most first
drafts. These are his notes, quoted as he wrote them, on drafts of sessions
27 and 28 that he sent back, followed by the way he would have told session
27's fight. This is the standard. Nothing below is a rule to follow. It is his
judgment, applied to real passages, so that you can apply it yourself.

## On a draft as a whole

> It is just a glorified list of line items from the session log. What was the
> core through line that was identified for this story? It seems to just be a
> list of every single action they took in chronological order along with some
> historical background on each. The reason the entry is too long is because
> it is saying far too much.

## On a later draft of the same session

> Both of these are not very impressive. The hag fight story is, again, just a
> line by line translation of my session notes, not a story.

## On an opening sentence, "Artus Cimber, who sailed with them out of Waterdeep"

> I don't think we need to keep re-introducing characters. Each chronicle entry
> is just another new page in the overall narrative. Artus was a topic just
> last week. Such a re-introduction would only make sense if it was a
> character that hasn't been referred to for a long time, or if the re-intro
> brought up topics relevant to the story being told in this particular entry.

## On "Shago, the guide hired at Fort Beluarian"

> That also seems silly. Shago has been with the group for multiple months now.
> He does not need an intro.

## On a first paragraph that ended with two sentences about the pterafolk and the hag reaching for a hair

> The entire first paragraph is an aside from the main bit of story for this
> session. Clearly the big event of this session was the hag fight and then the
> notification that the soulmonger was online. Anything else mentioned would
> need to be related to those stories, or quickly addressed. How are either of
> these sentences relevant to the story being told? They are not narratively
> linked to anything here, so I do not understand why the author wrote it.

## On "Nanny Pu'pu saw them on the trail and dressed the stage before they reached the top: a wounded man crawling toward them begging for help"

> Doesn't this bury the entire story? The crux of the story is that they were
> fooled into thinking they were helping this guy, and it turns out it was the
> hag. But the story doesn't run up to that as a crescendo, it just dumps it
> in the first sentence that it was part of her staging.

## On a `silent_roll` line, "Travel 20 to 24 Marpenoth, the fight on 25 Marpenoth, travel to Port Nyanzaru 25 Marpenoth to 6 Uktar"

> This is not the point of the silent roll. The players are not going to be
> looking at the list to see what dates they travelled on. The point of the
> silent roll is to list out significant things that happened that did not
> make it into the story of the chronicle.

## On a `silent_roll` line that began "19 Marpenoth, at camp:"

> Same thing here. The note is fine, but we don't need the date pulled through.

## On a `silent_roll` line, "Salida was hired for 150 gp, Chumble Crudluck and Lilac Mist doing the talking, so she can be questioned outside town and likely killed"

> The note would just be that they hired Salida. Their intentions and who
> spoke are not important enough for the silent roll.

## On the transition into a draft's last paragraph, which began "Frost giants patrolled the road to Port Nyanzaru"

> There is no transition between the hag fight and the frost giants. That is
> really jarring. Without a transition, it just looks like "oh, I forgot to
> include an item from the list."

## On a first paragraph that opened on why the company had come to Tamalka and then went on "Wickles went in first to look"

> The intro paragraph is setting up the scene then just suddenly drops into a
> blow by blow from the session log "Wickles went in first to look." The
> entire remainder of that paragraph does not fit with the first sentences of
> it. How is that proper paragraph structure?

## On "When it was over there was no one left to ask what they had wanted," followed by "In the wreckage was a signet ring cut with a scaled claw, a mark neither Salida nor Shago knew"

> "When it was over there was no one left to ask what they had wanted." feels
> like the end of the story, but we immediately jump into something that seems
> unworthy of mention. This still reads like a one to one line by line reading
> of the session log with extra words.

## On "the one who looked to be in charge turned to face them," where the log had "the one that appeared to be the leader turned and told them"

> This is literally my exact words from the session log!

## On a plan for session 28 shown as the log's events sorted into paragraphs

> That didn't look like a prospective story. That looked like a
> regurgitation of my notes from the session log.

## On a plan's through-line, "Someone else wanted what the company came to Tamalka for, and got there first," and a later one that said the strangers' question was the company's

> How do you know they want the same thing?

> How do you know the question being asked is the one the party brought?

## On a planned first paragraph about the letter Chumble Crudluck sent in with Wickles

> Who cares about the letter? That is a trivial part of the story that is
> irrelevant. It was essentially a joke by one of the players that I just
> didn't want to forget. Why would it be here?

## On a planned paragraph, "The ground and the archers. Pits, the two archers and the veteran fall"

> "The ground"? Are you referring to the traps? When talking to me, use
> normal language, not weird metaphors. Also, why would you note the number
> of enemy types that fell? When a Tolkien recounts a battle, does he have
> a line that says "and then they killed 3 wizards, 2 golems, and 1 ent"?

## On a planned paragraph that had Wickles sent ahead into the maze

> I do not see why Wickles being sent first would be notable enough to
> mention.

## On a silent roll that listed a jade statue and a scroll

> Why the statue and scroll? Why would the history book need that? We
> already track their inventory!

## On "the veteran," where the log's kill table said Veteran

> How does Volo know the other was a "veteran"? A veteran of what? In the
> session log, it is a reference to a specific creature type, but in the
> context of the story, what would a "veteran" mean?

## On the next plan, which then called the catoblepas "the raiders' beast"

> They know it was a catoblepas. My comment about veteran earlier was
> specifically "how does a player know a veteran from an archer from a
> fighter or gladiator". That doesn't extend to the catoblepas. They
> obviously know the difference between that and other creatures.

## How he would have told the fight

Read this against the log for session 27 and notice everything in it that the
log does not have. The skins and bone when the hut comes apart. The golem
shaking off dirt. Grieg closing his eyes and the scent forming before his nose
follows it. The word she spits when she is found. None of it is in the log,
and none of it is invented: the hut is the module's hut, the golem is the
module's golem, the nose is a feat on Grieg's sheet. He wrote from the record.

> After trekking back up the switchback trail of Mbala, they were greeted with
> a horrific sight. Hunched in front of Nanny's hut, crawling on his hands and
> knees, was a gravely wounded man crying for help as the hag stood behind
> him. Her eyes cast up and caught sight of the group, and silently turned and
> walked casually into the hut as though nothing was wrong. Grieg rushed to
> the man's aid as Urida launched a shatter into the hut to attempt to destroy
> the foul creature that had caused so much pain and suffering. The hut
> exploded in tattered animal skins and fragments of old bone, but there was
> no sight of the crone. Instead they heard her voice calling out, taunting
> them. As Grieg attempted to pull the man to safety, a hand burst from the
> ground, followed by the hulking figure of a flesh golem. As it shook off the
> dirt and debris, it attacked. The voice called out to "drop it!" and one of
> the flying monkeys dropped a bottle, glistening with some exotic insect
> creatures, and it burst into lightning bolts that struck the golem and seemed
> to energize it. The party fought through it all, they hacked at the golem
> before Chumble finally brought it down with a mighty eldritch blast. The hag
> revealed her presence once again and Lilac instinctively fired on it, only
> to reveal it as an illusion, setting the party to hunting around to find the
> elusive hag. Grieg, though, simply channelled his blood hunter senses, closed
> his eyes and thought back to seeing the foul beast just a tenday ago. The
> scent formed in his mind, and his nose followed it... right to the man at
> his feet. "Damn," she spat, and immediately dropped the facade, attacking
> with her claws. But the jig was up, and her time had run out. With the
> heroes all staring her down, she tried to go invisible and sneak away, only
> to feel a fireball from Urida in the back and the spear of Shago in the gut.

That is one scene, and it is most of the page. The session's main event gets
told like that, in full, and it gets most of the words. The session's other
event of weight gets what is left. Everything else is a clause on the way
somewhere, or a line in `silent_roll`, or nothing.
